KUT UX review

How KUT could work better on a phone, and where Midweek Madness should live. A ten-minute read. Nothing is built yet: pick a direction and the mockups follow.

1 Oct 2026. Taken from the app as it is on main (a472b4a) at 390 px and 1440 px, signed in as an ordinary member on a local stack. The Midweek week in the screenshots is invented: 21 entrants, as on 30 Sep, with made-up names and clock times.

Where Midweek should live

Its own tab, made by turning Leaderboard into the club's competition tab.

Midweek, Standings and Players as its sections, at /midweek. Still five tabs.

The biggest problem overall

The app doesn't lead with what's happening now.

On a Wednesday night, the live round is the third thing on Home, under a 390 px masthead.

The MM 2.0 evening

One page with a clock as its spine.

Your match first, blue left and red right, other matches shown at full time.

1 · The seven problems that matter most

Ranked by how much they cost a member over a normal week: Monday and Friday football, a pack when the coins allow, Wednesday night.

1Home doesn't lead with what's happening now

/every visit

What a member sees. On a phone, Home opens with a kicker, a 48 px "This week in KUT", the subtitle "Kelderklasse Ultimate Team", a paragraph about risers and two links. That's 390 px before anything you can act on. Then "Open a pack", and only then the Midweek card. At 20:50 on a Wednesday, with round 2 just out, the live evening is the third block on the page. The same goes for the +50-coin session report and the injury check-in, the two other things on Home with a deadline.

Why it matters. Home is where every link from the group chat lands. It's the only page every member sees every visit, and it currently spends its best space on things that never change. The nav audit compressed the header on Market, Collection and Packs; Home kept the desktop masthead.

Direction. A short header, then a "now" stack ordered by deadline: the Midweek evening while it runs, otherwise the earliest deadline among picking, the report and the check-in. Then risers, then activity. "Open a pack" stays a button, just not above a live event.

2Midweek Madness has no place of its own

/club/midweekweekly, plus Wednesday night

What a member sees. On the picker, the bracket and every match report, the tab bar lights Home. The way in is a Home card and a strip on Collection. The Leaderboard and the Chronicle never mention Midweek, and there is no list of past weeks: last week's bracket survives only as a one-line strip until the next lock.

Why it matters. It's the only part of KUT with a weekly deadline and a live evening, and it's what members will share in the group chat. A shared link that lands on a page lit "Home" doesn't teach anyone where Midweek lives. MM 2.0 makes this worse: the draw from 19:55, live matches and ratings all add surfaces. The options are in section 2.

3The inbox is a dead end

/messagesafter every event

What a member sees. Each message is a 200 px card with its own "Mark read" button, so a phone shows two and a half per screen. Nothing links to what the message is about: not the sale, not the offer, not the Chronicle issue the message mentions, not the Midweek bracket. A member who went out without winning a match gets no Midweek message at all, because ADR-096 only messages members who were paid.

Why it matters. The inbox is the app's only "this happened to you" channel. In the first week, 5 of 16 result messages had been read 40 minutes after the final, and the 5 entrants who won nothing were never told. If opening a message leads nowhere, members stop opening them.

Direction. Every message links to its subject, and opening one marks it read. Compact two-line rows, grouped by day. "Mark all read" stays.

4The picker hides the five slots, and its own Save bar covers them

/club/midweekKB-028KB-029

What a member sees. On a phone: the kicker, the title, the lock bar, a privacy line and an auto-squad notice come before "Your five" (about 570 px down). The sticky Save bar already overlaps slots 1 and 2 when the page loads. Below the slots, every card you own is a full-size LiveCard with a button: 17 Players make a 4,100 px page, sorted by OVR, with no archetype filter.

On desktop (KB-029), the countdown "in 1 day" floats top right beside the fairness seal and reads as the seal's, and the Save bar is a wide empty box with the button pushed to the far right. KB-028: a Player whose archetype changes next week says so only in the phone's slot rows. The desktop team sheet and the card grid at every width show only this week's archetype.

Why it matters. 17 of 21 members picked in the first week, and they picked by archetype: 39% of picked cards were All-rounders, against about 80% of the roster. The picker should be built for that. Squad shape needs a compact list and an archetype filter, not a long scroll of full cards.

5The evening stands still between reveals, and goes quiet once you're out

/club/midweek/[week]Wednesday night

What a member sees. After the lock, the bracket page tells you the bracket is drawn and "every match is already decided", then shows nothing until round 1. Between reveals nothing moves. Once you're out, your page is "+0 · Round 1 · out on penalties" and a link to next week.

Why it matters. MM 2.0 has already decided most of the fix: the draw from the lock, chance-by-chance matches and 15-minute rounds. The design job is to make that one page worth keeping open, and to give the 14 of 22 members who are out by about 20:20 a reason to stay until the final at 21:00. Section 3 sketches it.

6Long phone pages give you no way to find yourself

bracketmatch report

What a member sees. The phone bracket is 3,300 px during the evening and 5,200 px once complete (with pick shares). Your own match is wherever the draw put it, and there's no jump to it. A match report is 3,400 px, of which the "Why" panel takes about 2,000. In the report, whose chance it is appears only as small grey text ("Member B's chance"). In the bracket, "Member B" truncates to "Membe…" beside its YOU and BYE chips.

Why it matters. These are the pages a member opens on the night, one-handed, to answer two questions: how did I do, and who's next? Both answers are buried. The "Why" panel is the traceability promise of §44 (goal 4) and should stay, but behind a tap.

7A pack ends without saying what it did to your collection

/club/packs/[id]every pack

What a member sees. The reveal itself works: a tier tease, tap to flip, "Skip all". The summary then stacks three full cards (1,900 px) under "Your new Live Cards". Nothing says which card is new to your album and which is a third copy, or what that copy would discard for. The only mention of duplicates is on the confirm sheet: "Duplicates can happen."

Why it matters. Collecting all 29 is KUT's long game (the Collection header shows "17 / 29"), and opening a pack is where that number moves. The moment that should feel best currently ends on a plain list.

Direction. A "New · fills slot 14" or "×3 · discards for 63" chip on each card, using the LiveCard badge slot from ADR-102, and one summary line: "2 new Players. Album 19 / 29."

Option C · recommended

Leaderboard becomes the club's competition tab

The fifth tab (working name Compete, yours to name) gets three sections: Midweek · Standings · Players. Midweek comes first and moves to /midweek, with a redirect from /club/midweek. /leaderboard and /players keep their URLs and become Standings and Players. The tab carries a status in words as well as a mark: "not picked" before the lock, "LIVE" during the evening. Past weeks get a list under Midweek. MM 2.0's season table or knockout follow-up can live here later.

Gains

  • Midweek gets a tab that lights, a short URL to share, and room for every MM 2.0 screen.
  • Still five tabs. The longest label drops from 11 letters to 7.
  • Competition and standings sit together: the club's two "how am I doing against the others" surfaces.

Costs

  • "Leaderboard" leaves the bar. The Club Value table is one tap deeper; Home's Rank tile still links straight to it.
  • Reverses owner decision D1 and amends ADR-053, ADR-097 and BUILD_SPEC §46. That means a new ADR, frontend only.
  • Players (the card directory) is a slightly odd tenant. It's scouting, so it can stay.

Recommendation

Option C. Midweek is the only part of KUT that runs on a clock, and the evening is the one time members will open the app together. That needs a place that lights up, a URL people can paste into the group chat, and a tab that can say "live". The Club Value table is a monthly glance rather than a weekly habit, so it's the cheapest thing to move one tap deeper. And C keeps ADR-053's five tabs, which B can't.

Whichever you pick, Home keeps its Midweek card as the "now" entry (problem 1), and the Collection strip (D2) goes: one entry per state is enough.

Where I disagree with the earlier navigation audit

  • Home's lead (audit #8). The audit made the latest Chronicle issue Home's lead block. I'd lead with whatever has a clock (Midweek, the open report, the injury check-in) and put the Chronicle second. It's a weekly read, not a deadline.
  • The freed tab (#2, ADR-053). The audit gave the slot freed by /club to the Leaderboard. I'd now give it to the competition, with standings inside it.
  • Editorial headers (#21). The audit kept full headers on editorial pages, and Home kept the desktop masthead. On a phone Home is the most utilitarian page in the app.
  • The account menu (#10, #11). The avatar now links straight to Settings rather than opening a menu or a sheet. That's simpler and right. Only §46, which still describes a menu, needs updating.
  • Still open from the audit, and still right: the duplicate KUT Coins tile under the coin pill (#18), and capping Club activity at six rows (#23).

3 · A direction for the MM 2.0 evening

The feel: a match night in your pocket. One page, /midweek, that changes through the evening, with the clock as its spine. You glance at it, put the phone down, and glance again. It's built on the evening as decided: lock 19:55, rounds at 20:00, 20:15, 20:30, 20:45 and the final at 21:00, a chance every 20 s, a kick every 5 s, polling and no push.

  • The clock is the page. A sticky strip of stops (Lock · R1 · R2 · QF · SF · F). The current stop is ringed and named in words, and each stop jumps to its round.
  • Your match first, then this round's other matches, then the bracket. Once you're out, the final takes your match's place.
  • Blue left, red right, and always in words. Side 0 is blue and on the left everywhere: scoreboard, timeline and "Why". On a phone the timeline stays one column, but each event leans to its side (a blue rail on the left, or a red rail on the right) and names the manager. Position and name carry the meaning, colour adds to it.
  • Other matches never get ahead of anyone. They show "In play" until their full time (about 20:05 for round 1, later after a shoot-out), then the result. Never a live score.
  • Polling, shown quietly. A new chance slides in and the score ticks. "Updated 20:03:40" sits small under the clock. Reduced motion swaps the slide for a plain update.
  • Long shoot-outs. A sudden-death run can reach 50 kicks (4:10 at 5 s). The tally should grow in place and keep the score readable, not scroll the page.

5 · Questions for you

Only the decisions I can't make myself.

  1. Placement: A, B or C? I recommend C. If C, what's the tab called? "Compete" is a working name; "Matchday" or something the club already says may suit TFH better. It has to fit about 64 px at 320 px.
  2. Move Midweek to /midweek, with permanent redirects from /club/midweek? This reverses D1, so it needs your word and an ADR.
  3. Should every entrant get a result message, including members who won nothing? Today ADR-096 only messages members who were paid, so 5 of 21 heard nothing on 30 Sep. If not, the Home card and the share image are the only way they hear about it.
  4. The Groundmasters mini card: deferred until you pick a card direction. The mockups will reserve a Special variant of MidweekMiniCard that keys off the edition type. If you've already picked a direction, name it and I'll mock it.

Once you've picked, step 2 mocks the chosen direction at 320 px, 412 px and desktop in design/ux-review/, with a HANDOFF.md. That covers the picker (KB-028, KB-029), the draw from the lock, your match and the final live, a match you're not in before and after full time, the clock, whose chance it is, and Home's "now" stack. Ratings, the post-knockout follow-up and the share image stay marked placeholders.
